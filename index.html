For photographers, videographers and small studios

Your NAS, as fast and dependable as a local drive.

NasFast puts the NAS you already own into Finder, with Windows Explorer in preview. Browse huge shoots instantly, take a project offline, ingest cards that survive a dropped connection, and reach your files from anywhere. Your originals never leave the NAS.

Why not just use SMB?

SMB is fine for big copies on a fast LAN. It's the rest of the day where it hurts.

Browsing big folders

Thousands of RAW files in one folder means waiting on every visit. NasFast keeps a metadata cache, so revisiting a folder is immediate and changes on the NAS arrive as live updates.

Leaving the studio

SMB has no idea which files you'll need on the road. Pin a project and NasFast downloads it, verifies every byte and tells you honestly when it's fully available offline.

Working remotely

SMB over a VPN is chatty and fragile. NasFast is built on HTTPS with parallel range requests and reconnects on its own when your network changes.

What it does

Native in Finder

Your NAS shares appear in Finder's sidebar through Apple's File Provider. Files download on demand, so a 10 TB share costs almost nothing on your Mac until you open something. A Windows Explorer client is in preview.

Offline projects

Pin a folder to keep it on your SSD. Every chunk is checked against the NAS before NasFast calls the project available.

Safe editing

Edits are checked out with a lock on the NAS, uploaded as changed parts only, and committed atomically. If someone else changed the file, you keep your copy instead of silently overwriting theirs.

Resumable ingest

Uploads resume after an interruption and only count as done once they're durable on the NAS.

Your NAS permissions

On Synology, people sign in as their DSM accounts and NasFast enforces the same folder permissions, checked on the NAS for every request.

"Why is this slow?"

Instead of a spinner, NasFast tells you the likely cause: drives waking up, the NAS processor at its limit, a slow connection or a busy Mac.

Remote access you own

Use your router's port forward and dynamic DNS, or a private network like Tailscale. No vendor cloud account, relay or subscription is involved.

Several NAS boxes

Each NAS and share is independent. One being offline never interrupts another.

How it works

A small companion server runs on your NAS next to your existing folders. Your computers talk to it over mutually authenticated HTTPS.

  1. Install the companion on the NAS. It runs as an ordinary, unprivileged service and reads the folders you choose. Your files stay in their normal folders, in their normal formats.
  2. Claim it from your computer. On first start the NAS shows a short setup phrase. Type it into the NasFast app on the same local network and that computer becomes the owner.
  3. Invite your other devices. Each device gets its own certificate from your NAS. Revoke a lost laptop and it's locked out.
  4. Open your shares in Finder. NasFast picks the fastest working route on its own: the local network at home, your public address or Tailscale when you're away.

Security without a cloud in the middle

  • Each NAS is its own certificate authority. Nothing is shared with us or any other service.
  • Every connection uses TLS with a device certificate, and the app checks the NAS's exact certificate fingerprint.
  • New devices can only be enrolled from your local network, with a one-time phrase.
  • The internet-facing port refuses any connection that doesn't present an enrolled device certificate.
  • The companion never runs as root. On Synology, a small separate helper asks DSM for each permission decision.
  • Permission changes on the NAS apply to the very next request. Nothing is cached.
  • Revoking a device or a person takes effect immediately.
  • No telemetry. Diagnostics bundles are created only when you ask, with names and addresses removed.

Project status

NasFast is an early pilot built around one photographer's real workflow. It works day to day on one Synology NAS, but it isn't ready for anyone else's originals yet.

Working

  • Mac app and Finder integration
  • Offline projects
  • Safe editing and resumable uploads
  • DSM account permissions
  • Remote access over your own router

In progress

  • Windows Explorer client (preview)
  • Validation on more Synology models
  • Installer and automatic start on the NAS
  • Sleep, network-change and restart testing

Not planned

  • Replacing your backups
  • Hosting Lightroom Classic catalogs or other app databases over the network
  • Storing your files in someone else's cloud

NasFast is an independent project and isn't affiliated with Synology, Apple or Microsoft.
